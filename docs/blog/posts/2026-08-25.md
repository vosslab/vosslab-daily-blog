# A Preview Is a Promise

Today I kept returning to an unfashionable question: what, exactly, is a piece of software allowed to promise? I enjoyed work that replaced a plausible-looking answer with a durable one -- the specific curriculum being adopted, the actual molecule being edited, the one maintained policy behind several published documents. <!-- evidence: ev-0cc79e212b929d0d, ev-ecdaafed6eb2e135, ev-18f41c4d87a42956 -->

## The proposed change has to survive contact with reality

In [`vosslab/peptidyle-learning-engine`](https://github.com/vosslab/peptidyle-learning-engine), I worked on the foundation for curriculum adoption. That phrase can make the work sound like copying content from one course into another, but the copy is not the important part. A curriculum has identity: the exact publication pin, authored ordering, pools, defaults, schedules, and target-term interpretation all help define what an instructor is actually adopting. <!-- evidence: ev-0cc79e212b929d0d -->

The detail I found most satisfying was the answer-free preview. Before a write, the system explains the intended destination without exposing answers. A later apply operation derives from that exact preview and may fast-forward only if the preview still qualifies. If the world has changed, the recovery types constrain the next move instead of allowing the application to perform something merely adjacent to what the instructor reviewed. <!-- evidence: ev-0cc79e212b929d0d -->

That is a small conceptual reversal. A preview is often treated as a courtesy screen -- useful, but disposable. Here it becomes part of the authority chain. It says, in effect: this is the change I showed you, this is the state it was based on, and this is the only change I am now entitled to make. Structural equality and a canonical digest give that promise a stable form that can be checked later. <!-- evidence: ev-0cc79e212b929d0d -->


The gratifying part is that the local contracts are real: focused Rust tests, doctests, TypeScript compilation, formatting, strict Clippy, source checks, Markdown links, and diff hygiene all passed. The important unfinished part is equally real. The final B2 claim remains open until the Store, PostgreSQL, browser, and evidence slices run together against one final material tree. I do not want "the pieces compile" to become a substitute for "the adoption happened correctly." <!-- evidence: ev-0cc79e212b929d0d, ev-08997bf317865457 -->

## One maintained truth can wear several forms

[`vosslab/syllabus`](https://github.com/vosslab/syllabus) brought the same idea into a more public setting. I made Fall 2026 material for BIOL 318/418, BIOL 351/451, and BIOL 480 derive from Markdown sources that can produce the site, DOCX, PDF, and optional term archive. The goal was not to force every output through one renderer. It was to make the editable material singular even when the delivery formats need different technical paths. <!-- evidence: ev-18f41c4d87a42956 -->

The grading scale is the simplest proof. A threshold such as `92.0% and above` should be written once, not copied into several course pages and later rediscovered as several almost-identical policies. Shared source material is embedded as restricted Markdown fragments, so it can appear where students need it without becoming a private fork inside every course. <!-- evidence: ev-18f41c4d87a42956 -->

I liked the practical stubbornness of the exporter work. Pandoc remains appropriate for DOCX, while Python-Markdown and WeasyPrint form the PDF path. A print-site-plugin experiment was rejected because it produced a blank contents page, duplicate pagination, and a longer document. That was useful evidence, not wasted effort: one editorial authority does not require one rendering implementation. It requires every renderer to begin from the same maintained material. <!-- evidence: ev-18f41c4d87a42956 -->

The release path got the same treatment. Generated downloads are staged and checked as a complete PDF/DOCX set before replacing the existing files. The remaining design question is Spring 2027: how to retain Fall 2026 as an immutable record without quietly creating a second editable authority. I would rather leave that question visible than solve it with an archive that only looks stable. <!-- evidence: ev-18f41c4d87a42956 -->

## The boundary is where the work becomes useful

In [`vosslab/ferrum-chemical-forge`](https://github.com/vosslab/ferrum-chemical-forge), the authority problem is lower-level and less forgiving. The completed `document_object_id` migration means the Rust document allocates opaque identities and projection, rendering, properties, export, reactions, selection, clipboard handling, and CLI behavior carry them forward. Qt no longer gets to reconstruct which document object a source ID, render key, or local ordering might have meant. <!-- evidence: ev-ecdaafed6eb2e135 -->

Compact groups made that feel concrete. An attached `NO2` is not adequately proven by a dialog that closes or an icon that appears. The materialized result needs to be an editable graph with its atoms, bonds, composition, formal charge, history behavior, and reopening behavior intact. Rust owns validation, chemistry capacity, durable-ID allocation, history, and atomic commit; Qt owns interaction and the accessible presentation of receipts and typed refusals. <!-- evidence: ev-ecdaafed6eb2e135 -->

Several smaller projects gave me versions of the same satisfaction. [`vosslab/qti-package-maker`](https://github.com/vosslab/qti-package-maker) now refuses an empty bank before it creates a misleading output file, separates HTML self-test controls by item CRC, and rejects Boolean or non-finite numeric answers. [`vosslab/screenshot-ai-renamer-macos`](https://github.com/vosslab/screenshot-ai-renamer-macos) narrows a verbose model response into `<filename>` XML while retaining a sanitizer fallback, making its chosen Ollama model a configuration authority rather than a per-call suggestion. <!-- evidence: ev-44f2ea36336431ed, ev-d1975a09ed44d196 -->

[`vosslab/biology-problems-website`](https://github.com/vosslab/biology-problems-website) now owns the ordinary route to a local preview through `run_web_server.sh`, and it ships its reading font instead of asking Google for one. [`vosslab/biology-problems`](https://github.com/vosslab/biology-problems) went in the opposite direction where restraint was right: generated ordinary text stopped imposing Arial and generic sans-serif choices, allowing a host course site to supply its own normal typography. <!-- evidence: ev-51daef1c9ba76fe3, ev-ce598d3b8b5ebe23 -->

[`vosslab/virtual-lab-protocol-simulation`](https://github.com/vosslab/virtual-lab-protocol-simulation) preserved `tools/normalize_svg_v3.py` as a stable command while moving the implementation to `tools/svg_normalizer/`; [`vosslab/starter-repo-template`](https://github.com/vosslab/starter-repo-template) preserved whole dated changelog blocks and rejects an oversized day before it can create a dishonest archive. Both changes keep a stable edge while making actual ownership easier to find. <!-- evidence: ev-645410a4accf0f87, ev-306feb84aa3d91e5 -->

I ended with [`vosslab/battery-control`](https://github.com/vosslab/battery-control), which did not claim an explanation it does not yet have. Its extended hourly ledger makes a particular anomaly inspectable: at 2026-08-24 20:00, a listed price of `4.9` sits below a cutoff of `5.1`, yet the state remains `above_cutoff` with a target of 40 and charge falling from 88% to 74%. The next hour returns to `below_cutoff` while charge falls again. <!-- evidence: ev-9e61f42febb68c36 -->

That is a good ending for the day. The ledger has not solved the puzzle, but it has made the ambiguity precise enough to investigate: policy logic, timing, hysteresis, or telemetry semantics. A system earns trust not by always producing an answer, but by making its unanswered questions clear. <!-- evidence: ev-9e61f42febb68c36 -->
