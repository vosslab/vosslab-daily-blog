# Authority Must Be Current

Today I kept running into the same design question in different clothes: what information may persist, and what permission has to be renewed at the moment it matters? The answer became most concrete in teaching software, where a person can be approved to teach without yet being entitled to teach in every course. That sounds like a narrow distinction until an invitation is accepted, a group changes, or the last Instructor tries to leave. Then it becomes the whole shape of the system.
<!-- evidence: ev-8b866d69dddd5211 -->

<!-- more -->

## Approval is not membership

Most of the day went into [vosslab/peptidyle-learning-engine](https://github.com/vosslab/peptidyle-learning-engine) and its WP-PROF-T2 teaching-operations work. I wanted operator approval to mean exactly one thing: an operator has established that this person is eligible to instruct. It must not quietly grant teaching power in every course. Actual authority comes from an explicit Instructor membership in the particular course.
<!-- evidence: ev-8b866d69dddd5211 -->

That separation made the awkward cases much clearer. A co-instructor invitation is addressed to one recipient, lasts 30 days, and turns into ordinary direct membership only after approval is checked again. Leaving a course is refused when it would remove the final active Instructor. Course-group changes can alter current S5/S3 applicability atomically, but they do not rewrite sealed receipt generations. I enjoyed that constraint: the present policy can be alive without lying about what a learner was issued under yesterday's policy.
<!-- evidence: ev-8b866d69dddd5211 -->

It also pushed responsibility out of the browser. Public `U-`, `M-`, and `CI-` locators keep raw identity out of the public surface; account lookup stays session-bound, membership lookup stays exact-course and Instructor-bound, and bounded snapshots omit emails and UUIDs. The server owns revision checks, preview boundaries, retention, and denial. That is less clever than reconstructing policy in a client, but it gives the software one place to answer the hard question.
<!-- evidence: ev-8b866d69dddd5211 -->

## A visible denial is part of the feature

The paired assignment previews became my favorite small piece of the work. An allowed screen is reassuring, but a denied screen explains whether the policy is doing real work or merely hiding an option. A readable instructor-facing outcome makes a boundary inspectable without exposing its machinery.
<!-- evidence: ev-d7af3ef8de333a7c, ev-f02ff4739a007c50, ev-8b866d69dddd5211 -->



The validation record was gratifying because it traced the idea through typed domain code, Memory and PostgreSQL projections, migrations, roles, server behavior, browser tests, and the local-stack lifecycle. It records 301 Node tests, 5,481 pytest tests, and 245 built Playwright tests with no skips, plus a fresh 43-migration PostgreSQL baseline. The numbers are not the story; they matter because the authority boundary survived each place where it could have been casually widened.
<!-- evidence: ev-8b866d69dddd5211 -->

## Installations are projections too

The same thought showed up in [vosslab/vosslab-skills](https://github.com/vosslab/vosslab-skills). I stripped away installer profiles, receipts, ownership hashes, version reconciliation, pruning state, and hidden backups. The Git checkout is the authority; an installed platform directory is a replaceable projection. The installer compares selected destination entries with the repository, leaves matching entries alone, and replaces mismatches from Git.
<!-- evidence: ev-c7c8d92c646b35b3 -->

That does not flatten the platforms into one pretend standard. Canonical skills and authored Claude agents can remain live through symlinks, while target formats that have no authored source remain deliberate generated files. Claude, Codex, Cursor, and OpenCode keep their distinct destination shapes. What disappeared was the fiction that a receipt can become a competing source of truth.
<!-- evidence: ev-c7c8d92c646b35b3 -->

[vosslab/secure-agent-playbook-generic](https://github.com/vosslab/secure-agent-playbook-generic) arrived at a closely related boundary from the other direction. It keeps OWASP material in its established layout and places portability in installer, discovery, conversion, manifest, resolver, and harness-contract adapters. The important correction is practical: a portable installed skill includes the transitive closure of its cited local datasets and assets, with rewritten links that resolve from its own `references/data/` tree. A copied Markdown file without the evidence it cites is not a portable skill in any useful sense.
<!-- evidence: ev-48f45984d3790a49 -->

The fork also gives local edits their proper weight. A saved profile can remember installation topology, but an ordinary rerun preserves changed managed files; replacing one requires a fresh `--force`. Remembering where something goes is not standing permission to overwrite later work. That is the same lesson as course authority, made quiet and mundane at a filesystem boundary.
<!-- evidence: ev-48f45984d3790a49 -->

## Educational artifacts need traceable seams

The educational publishing work gave me a useful next question rather than a tidy conclusion. [vosslab/biology-problems-website](https://github.com/vosslab/biology-problems-website) published Topic 01 through source question text, a self-test manifest, readable HTML, browser self-tests, PGML downloads, Blackboard exports, and Canvas/ADAPT QTI packages. The material covers functional groups, bond types, macromolecules, hydrophobicity, and course-scoped variants; Topic 02 continues into chemical groups, Henderson-Hasselbalch work, buffering, concentration ratios, and protonation-state questions.
<!-- evidence: ev-4f0b21279c1b31f5, ev-f803861b790636fc, ev-461166fe821026cb -->

I like that a question can meet learners where they work, but several file formats are only valuable if they still describe the same question. The evidence confirms conversion and deployment artifacts, not archive contents, LMS import results, or behavioral equivalence across formats. The next improvement is therefore not another export target. It is a testable contract joining source, catalog, browser self-test, readable page, and package.
<!-- evidence: ev-4f0b21279c1b31f5, ev-f803861b790636fc -->

In [vosslab/qti-package-maker](https://github.com/vosslab/qti-package-maker), I made a smaller structural version of that move: the stable `read_items_from_file()` interface now delegates to separate package orchestration, media recovery, and item-XML parsing modules. The HTML void-element repair test moved with the parser behavior it verifies. It is the kind of cleanup that should reduce internal ambiguity without asking downstream callers to learn a new story.
<!-- evidence: ev-9ed0007294cf1489 -->

## Keep the awkward measurements

[vosslab/battery-control](https://github.com/vosslab/battery-control) supplied the operational counterpart. Its hourly history now records price context, cutoff classification, reserve target, operating mode, and state-of-charge transitions. In the new rows, the log preserves two `STARTUP` entries and duplicate `2026-08-18 19:00` records rather than tidying them away before anyone has decided what they mean.
<!-- evidence: ev-a3954f4388631c0e, ev-724f170cabae07ff -->

That restraint is easy to miss. A clean aggregate can be comforting, but it can also erase the very condition that should change the conclusion. Before I make performance claims from this history, I need an explicit analysis rule for startup behavior and duplicate timestamps. An audit trail earns its place by retaining the irregularities that later analysis has to confront.
<!-- evidence: ev-a3954f4388631c0e, ev-724f170cabae07ff -->

The day's smallest deletion made the same point with more bluntness: [vosslab/track-runner-virtual-dolly-cam](https://github.com/vosslab/track-runner-virtual-dolly-cam) dropped a 1,061-line PyPI release utility because it should not live there. That narrows the maintenance boundary. It does not prove that release safeguards exist elsewhere, and I do not want to tell a more satisfying story than the evidence supports.
<!-- evidence: ev-11836c341a84d1d0 -->

What I want to carry forward is not a preference for fewer layers or stricter permissions in the abstract. It is the discipline of giving each fact and each authority a home, then making transitions explicit when one becomes the other. Peptidyle's professor-handoff work still has to preserve the distinction among eligibility, course authority, operational discovery, and ownership. The skills repository still has locally changed propagation-owned tests to resolve. Those are good unfinished problems: each tests whether today's clearer boundary stays clear when the next requirement presses on it.
<!-- evidence: ev-8b866d69dddd5211, ev-c7c8d92c646b35b3 -->
